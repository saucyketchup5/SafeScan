01EVERYDAY DIGITAL LIFE

Internet use is deeply integrated into students' lives.

With 92.92% regularly using the internet and 69.17% accessing it at home, online safety cannot be treated as a school-only issue. Study and school activities are the most common purpose at 66.67%, while social media, messaging, entertainment, games, and information searching also form part of students' online experiences.

02AWARENESS GAP

Knowing the terms does not always mean full understanding.

Although 59.58% have heard of OSAEC and 57.08% have heard of CSAEM, 26.25% remain unsure about each concept. This indicates a need for clear, age-appropriate explanations that connect terminology with recognizable online situations.

03HELP-SEEKING

Students value trusted people and clear pathways.

Most respondents know where to seek help, but fear, shame, and concern about reactions remain important barriers. Students also report greater comfort when they have clear information about where to go and when they expect the person they approach to listen, believe them, and provide actual help.

04PODCAST DIRECTION

Practical, relatable, and youth-friendly communication.

Students most often prefer real situations/examples (43.33%) and online safety tips (40.83%). Simple/casual discussion is the most selected discussion style at 41.25%. These findings point toward content built around realistic scenarios, youth-friendly language, practical safety tips, and clear reporting or referral information.

OVERALL INTERPRETATION

Move from awareness to practical action.

The combined findings support a youth-centered online safety and child protection approach that focuses not only on knowing what OSAEC and CSAEM are, but also on recognizing danger, protecting oneself, knowing who to approach, understanding how to report, and knowing how to support a friend.
